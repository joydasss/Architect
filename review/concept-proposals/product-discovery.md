Date: 2026-07-31

Proposed cross-cutting concept ownership entries for merge into CORE_CONCEPTS.md:

| Concept | Suggested Owning Chapter | Scope Note |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery/delivery track split (dual-track agile) | [Product Discovery](../../02_Design_Thinking_and_Product_Management/CH14_Product_Discovery.md) | Continuous, parallel discovery and delivery tracks sharing a team but not a queue; distinct from a one-time "discovery phase." |
| Four big risks framework (value, usability, feasibility, business viability) | [Product Discovery](../../02_Design_Thinking_and_Product_Management/CH14_Product_Discovery.md) | Cagan's risk taxonomy for evaluating whether an opportunity is safe to commit delivery capacity to. |
| Product trio (team topology) | [Product Discovery](../../02_Design_Thinking_and_Product_Management/CH14_Product_Discovery.md) | Standing PM/design/engineering trio as the durable unit of continuous discovery ownership, as an alternative to sequential handoff. |
| Continuous discovery cadence | [Product Discovery](../../02_Design_Thinking_and_Product_Management/CH14_Product_Discovery.md) | Protected weekly customer-touchpoint habit (Torres) that keeps discovery ongoing rather than episodic. |
| Opportunity assessment (risk-ranked assumption testing) | [Product Discovery](../../02_Design_Thinking_and_Product_Management/CH14_Product_Discovery.md) | Structured technique for naming assumptions under an opportunity and ranking them by importance x current evidence to choose what to test next; distinct from, and operates within, the Opportunity Solution Tree owned by Problem Framing. |
| Discovery artifacts (opportunity backlog, assumption map, discovery decision log) | [Product Discovery](../../02_Design_Thinking_and_Product_Management/CH14_Product_Discovery.md) | Decision-oriented, per-opportunity artifact set, distinct in purpose and lifespan from design-research artifacts (personas, journey maps, etc.). |

Notes:

- This chapter deliberately recaps rather than re-teaches four techniques
  owned by earlier Design Thinking subsection chapters: Research
  ([CH02](../../02_Design_Thinking_and_Product_Management/CH02_Research.md)),
  User Interviews
  ([CH04](../../02_Design_Thinking_and_Product_Management/CH04_User_Interviews.md)),
  Rapid Prototyping
  ([CH08](../../02_Design_Thinking_and_Product_Management/CH08_Rapid_Prototyping.md)),
  and Usability Testing
  ([CH10](../../02_Design_Thinking_and_Product_Management/CH10_Usability_Testing.md)).
  No new ownership claimed over any of these.
- This chapter also recaps, without re-teaching, the Opportunity Solution
  Tree owned by Problem Framing
  ([CH06](../../02_Design_Thinking_and_Product_Management/CH06_Problem_Framing.md)),
  and explicitly distinguishes it from this chapter's own opportunity
  assessment technique (single-opportunity risk/evidence ranking vs.
  outcome-to-opportunity-to-solution structural mapping).
- This chapter explicitly defers product strategy/vision-setting to the
  forthcoming Product Strategy chapter (CH15, drafted in the same batch,
  not yet visible to this session) and does not claim any strategy-level
  concept.
- This chapter was drafted without visibility into a possible parallel
  Lean UX chapter (CH13). If CH13 exists once this batch is reviewed,
  check specifically for overlap on: continuous validation cadence,
  assumption-driven prioritization, and MVP-adjacent language — this
  chapter deliberately avoided teaching MVP construction itself (deferred
  to the future "MVP" Product Management chapter per PART_OVERVIEW.md).
- No overlap expected with future Product Management chapters (Personas,
  Journey Mapping, Prioritization, Experimentation, A/B Testing, MVP) since
  this chapter deliberately avoided teaching prioritization frameworks
  (e.g., RICE/MoSCoW/Kano), experimentation statistics, or MVP construction
  in depth, using only generic "importance x evidence" ranking as its own
  narrow, opportunity-assessment-specific technique.
