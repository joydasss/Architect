# Part 23 — Soft Skills Topic Map

This map defines coverage for Part 23's fifteen professional-practice chapters. The part focuses on the interpersonal and organizational work that makes architecture decisions understandable, actionable, and accountable; it complements technical design rather than replacing it.

## Part-wide ownership boundaries

- Part 23 owns communication, collaboration, leadership, and planning practices for architects.
- Part 21 owns architecture-document formats and artifacts; Part 22 owns architecture reasoning and facilitated decision practice; Part 18 owns enterprise architecture governance.
- Examples should distinguish observable evidence from opinion, and should show how communication adapts to audience, authority, and decision stakes.

## Cross-part stakeholders and scenarios

- Architects coordinate executives, product owners, engineers, operations, finance, legal, vendors, and delivery leaders with competing incentives.
- Important scenarios include an executive decision under time pressure; unresolved technical disagreement; uncertain estimates; a delivery commitment at risk; and a vendor proposal with hidden lifecycle cost.

## Chapters

### CH01 Executive Communication

**Reader outcome:** Communicate architecture implications and recommendations in concise, decision-oriented language for executive audiences.
**Coverage:** Audience needs, framing, decision requests, evidence, risk, options, concise summaries, and follow-through.
**Diagram opportunities:** (1) technical evidence to business decision; (2) executive briefing structure; (3) option/risk/cost comparison; (4) decision authority and escalation path; (5) message adaptation by audience.

### CH02 Architecture Reviews

**Reader outcome:** Prepare and facilitate constructive reviews that test assumptions, surface risks, and produce clear decisions or actions.
**Coverage:** Review purpose, preparation, participants, evidence, challenge questions, decision recording, and follow-up; Part 22 owns the deeper decision-practice method.
**Diagram opportunities:** (1) review lifecycle; (2) architecture evidence map; (3) participant and decision-rights map; (4) issue-to-action tracking; (5) review finding severity and escalation.

### CH03 Presentation Skills

**Reader outcome:** Present architecture clearly using a coherent narrative, audience-appropriate visuals, and deliberate handling of questions.
**Coverage:** Narrative arc, slide and diagram selection, pacing, rehearsal, accessibility, question handling, and remote presentation.
**Diagram opportunities:** (1) context-problem-options-recommendation narrative; (2) visual density progression; (3) audience attention and detail layers; (4) question parking-lot and follow-up flow; (5) presentation feedback loop.

### CH04 Negotiation

**Reader outcome:** Reach durable agreements by separating interests from positions and making constraints and trade-offs explicit.
**Coverage:** Preparation, interests, alternatives, concessions, objective criteria, authority, ethics, and agreement confirmation.
**Diagram opportunities:** (1) positions-to-interests map; (2) option space and shared gains; (3) negotiation preparation canvas; (4) concession and approval boundaries; (5) agreement-to-owner-action trace.

### CH05 Conflict Resolution

**Reader outcome:** Address technical and interpersonal conflict early with fair process, evidence, and explicit resolution or escalation.
**Coverage:** Conflict signals, listening, reframing, causes, facilitation, psychological safety, escalation, and post-resolution learning.
**Diagram opportunities:** (1) conflict escalation curve; (2) positions/interests/evidence distinction; (3) resolution conversation flow; (4) escalation and mediation path; (5) agreement and follow-up loop.

### CH06 Stakeholder Management

**Reader outcome:** Identify stakeholders, understand their influence and concerns, and maintain useful engagement through architecture change.
**Coverage:** Stakeholder discovery, influence/impact, incentives, communication cadence, alignment, resistance, and ownership transitions.
**Diagram opportunities:** (1) influence/impact map; (2) stakeholder-to-concern matrix; (3) engagement timeline; (4) decision influence network; (5) change-impact and feedback flow.

### CH07 Technical Writing

**Reader outcome:** Write precise technical material that lets readers understand context, evidence, decisions, and required action.
**Coverage:** Audience, structure, terminology, evidence, diagrams, editing, accessibility, review, and maintenance; Part 21 owns artifact-specific formats.
**Diagram opportunities:** (1) claim-evidence-conclusion chain; (2) document information hierarchy; (3) reader journey through a design; (4) source-to-claim traceability; (5) review and revision workflow.

### CH08 Mentoring

**Reader outcome:** Help colleagues grow architectural judgment through guided practice, feedback, and increasing ownership.
**Coverage:** Goal setting, active listening, coaching questions, feedback, psychological safety, stretch work, and boundaries.
**Diagram opportunities:** (1) mentoring cycle; (2) skill-to-practice progression; (3) feedback observation-impact-action model; (4) support-versus-ownership continuum; (5) mentoring goals and evidence tracker.

### CH09 Leadership

**Reader outcome:** Lead architecture work through direction, trust, decision clarity, and responsibility without relying solely on formal authority.
**Coverage:** Influence, delegation, accountability, vision, team alignment, change leadership, ethics, and adaptive response.
**Diagram opportunities:** (1) vision-to-team-action cascade; (2) authority and influence map; (3) delegation and decision boundaries; (4) leadership response across change stages; (5) accountability and feedback loop.

### CH10 Hiring

**Reader outcome:** Build fair, role-relevant hiring processes that assess architectural capability through consistent evidence.
**Coverage:** Role definition, competency signals, structured interviews, work samples, calibration, bias mitigation, and candidate experience.
**Diagram opportunities:** (1) role outcomes to assessment map; (2) interview loop and evidence capture; (3) competency rubric; (4) candidate decision funnel; (5) bias checks across hiring stages.

### CH11 Estimation

**Reader outcome:** Produce estimates that communicate uncertainty, assumptions, dependencies, and confidence rather than false precision.
**Coverage:** Scope decomposition, reference classes, ranges, assumptions, risk, estimation methods, calibration, and estimate updates.
**Diagram opportunities:** (1) estimate range and confidence; (2) work breakdown and dependency graph; (3) uncertainty sources; (4) forecast versus actual calibration; (5) estimate update triggers.

### CH12 Planning

**Reader outcome:** Turn outcomes and constraints into an adaptive plan with dependencies, decision points, owners, and feedback.
**Coverage:** Outcomes, sequencing, capacity, dependencies, milestones, risk buffers, replanning, and cross-team coordination.
**Diagram opportunities:** (1) outcome-to-milestone roadmap; (2) dependency network and critical path; (3) decision gates; (4) capacity versus demand; (5) plan-inspect-adapt cycle.

### CH13 Budgeting

**Reader outcome:** Explain architecture-related costs and budget choices with transparent assumptions, lifecycle effects, and ownership.
**Coverage:** Capital/operating costs, run/build balance, forecasts, cost drivers, contingency, funding models, and variance response; Part 25 owns FinOps methods.
**Diagram opportunities:** (1) lifecycle cost profile; (2) budget allocation by capability; (3) forecast-to-actual variance; (4) funding decision and approval path; (5) cost ownership map.

### CH14 Risk Management

**Reader outcome:** Identify, prioritize, communicate, and track risks with proportionate treatment and accountable owners.
**Coverage:** Risk statements, likelihood/impact, exposure, mitigations, residual risk, escalation, review cadence, and opportunity risks.
**Diagram opportunities:** (1) risk register and ownership flow; (2) likelihood-impact matrix; (3) treatment and residual-risk path; (4) risk dependency network; (5) trigger-to-response timeline.

### CH15 Vendor Management

**Reader outcome:** Govern vendor relationships and technical dependencies across selection, contracting, delivery, operations, and exit.
**Coverage:** Evaluation criteria, due diligence, service commitments, integration, security, performance, relationship health, lock-in, and exit planning.
**Diagram opportunities:** (1) vendor lifecycle; (2) capability and evaluation matrix; (3) shared responsibility boundary; (4) service escalation path; (5) dependency, portability, and exit map.
